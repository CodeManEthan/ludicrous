    What is this?

    Ludicrous takes games meant for a kitchen table and runs them far past anything you could play for real: 1,000 players, 1,000 decks, hundreds of millions of rounds. The whole game is simulated first, in seconds; then you play it back like a video.

    War

    1. The decks are shuffled together and dealt round-robin to every player.
    2. Each round, every player turns over their top card.
    3. The highest card takes every card played that round.
    4. A tie for highest goes to war: each tied player puts 3 cards face down and one face up, and the highest of those takes the lot (a war can repeat).
    5. A player with no cards is out. The last player holding cards wins.

    Cards rank 2 (low) to Ace (high); suits don't matter, so with many decks two identical cards simply tie. Players and decks are set separately: 1,000 players on 20 decks get about one card each and most are out in round 1, while 256 decks give each player 13 cards and a long, crowded game.

    Cap rounds at (under Options) stops a game at that round. A capped game has no winner and reads unfinished. It's off by default, so every game plays to the end.

    Blackjack

    Up to 200 seats against one dealer (dealer stands on soft 17, blackjack pays 3:2, doubling and splitting allowed). Each seat plays a fixed strategy, given out round-robin from the ones you pick. Bankrolls are in units, one unit being one bet.

    • Basic strategy: the standard chart. Hit, stand, double or split by your hand and the dealer's up card. The best you can do without counting cards.
    • Never bust: hit only when no card can bust you, then stand.
    • Hit below 15 / 16 / 17: hit until the hand reaches that total, then stand, whatever the dealer shows.

    Batch

    Batch runs a thousand games (or ten thousand) and shows what happens on average. For War: how long games run, whether any seat wins more than its share, and the shortest and longest games, each of which you can replay. For Blackjack: each strategy's expected win or loss per hand.

    Watching a game

    • The winner, the chart's future and the outcome tiles stay hidden until playback reaches the end; Skip to results reveals them.
    • Highlights plays only the key moments: eliminations, the deepest war, the biggest pot, lead changes.
    • Slow the opening (under ⚙ Options) gives the crowded first rounds and each elimination real screen time, then spends the rest on the final duel.
    • Eliminated players can collapse into a strip, stay dimmed, hide after a while, or hide at once.
    • Keys: Space plays and pauses; ← → step one round.
    • Import… loads a game saved by the command-line simulator (simulate.py --json) and plays it back here.

    How it works

    War runs in your browser: the engine is written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly, and it runs in a background worker. It simulates the whole game once, keeps snapshots along the way, and rebuilds any round from the nearest snapshot, so scrubbing a hundred-million-round game is instant. Blackjack and Batch run on the server: Blackjack on the Python engine, War batches on the same Rust core built as a native program across every CPU core. Every game is seeded, so the same seed replays the same game.
